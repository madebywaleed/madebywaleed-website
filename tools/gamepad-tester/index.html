Stick drift and range

Drift: let go of both sticks for a second. A good stick rests close to 0. A resting offset above about 0.10 is drift, and games may move on their own.

Range: slowly roll each stick around its outer edge a few times. The blue shape should look like a circle. The error shows how far the edge is from a perfect circle; under 10% is normal, and a much larger number means a worn or uneven stick.

Good to know

  • Works with Xbox, PlayStation (DualSense and DualShock), Nintendo Switch Pro and most other controllers in Chrome, Edge and Firefox. Safari supports fewer controllers.
  • Browsers can't read motion sensors (gyro), touchpad movement, adaptive trigger resistance, the light bar or the headset jack, so those can't be tested here. The PlayStation touchpad click and most Home buttons do work.
  • If buttons look swapped or some are missing, the controller may be in a different mode (for example DirectInput instead of XInput) or need its own app. Try the other connection, USB or Bluetooth.
  • Several controllers can be connected at once. Pick one with the Controller buttons above.
  • Nothing is saved or sent anywhere.

How to test your controller

  1. Connect your controller with USB or Bluetooth, then press any button so the browser detects it.
  2. Press every button and trigger, and move both sticks through their full range.
  3. Let go of both sticks for a second to check for stick drift.
  4. Run the vibration and polling rate tests if you want to check those features too.

What each test checks

  • Controller info shows the controller it detected and the number of buttons and axes it reports.
  • Triggers show how far each trigger is pressed, from fully released to fully pressed.
  • Left stick and Right stick show the position of each stick and its movement.
  • Stick drift and range checks whether a stick rests at zero, and whether it moves in a smooth circle.
  • Buttons lights up each button as you press it, so you can spot a button that does not register.
  • Vibration lets you test the light and heavy motors, if your controller and browser support them.
  • Polling rate estimates how often the controller reports to the browser.
  • Double-press check flags a button that registers a second press too soon after release.
  • Axes shows the raw value of each axis, which is useful for technical checks.

Stick drift: what to look for

Stick drift means a stick reports movement when you are not touching it. Let go of both sticks and check the resting position. A good stick rests close to zero. A resting offset above about 0.10 is drift, and games may move your character or camera on their own.

To check the range, roll each stick slowly around its outer edge a few times. The shape should look like a circle. Under 10% error is normal. A much larger error suggests a worn or uneven stick.

Compatibility

  • Works with Xbox, PlayStation (DualSense and DualShock), Nintendo Switch Pro and most other controllers in Chrome, Edge and Firefox. Safari supports fewer controllers.
  • Several controllers can be connected at once. Choose the one you want to test with the controller buttons on the page.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to install drivers or an app?

Usually not. Connect the controller and the page will detect it. If buttons are missing or swapped, the controller's own app or mode setting may need changing.

Does it work with an Xbox controller?

Yes. Xbox controllers work in Chrome, Edge and Firefox over USB or Bluetooth.

What is stick drift?

Stick drift is when a stick reports movement while it rests in the centre. It is a common wear problem. Use the stick drift panel to check, and contact the manufacturer if the offset is clear.

Can I test two controllers at once?

Yes. Connect both controllers, then choose the one you want to view with the controller buttons on the page.

Is anything I press recorded?

No. Button presses and stick movements are not saved or sent anywhere.
